Bubble Up Endless - Tips, Tricks & How to Get a High Score

Bubble Up EndlessPuzzle · Free to play
Play now
Advertisement

Bubble Up Endless is a bubble shooter where one bad shot can snowball into a crowded board. These tips come from several runs, including one that ended at 3,250 points and one that reached 50,150.

  1. Aim at an existing group of the same color instead of attaching a bubble to an isolated one. Groups clear, loose bubbles just stay on the board and crowd you.
  2. Look for the bubbles holding up a big cluster. Clearing the support makes everything hanging from it fall at once, which gave me the biggest score jumps.
  3. Chase combos. The game shows Combo x2 and higher when you clear on back-to-back shots, and the score climbs much faster once the multiplier builds.
  4. Keep an eye on the red dashed line. When the bubble formation touches it you see FAILED, so clear from the bottom of the board before you go for fancy shots.
  5. Treat a miss as expensive. Missed shots add bubbles and push the formation down, so a safe small clear is often better than a risky long one.
  6. Check the progress counter at the top, which showed values like 51/230 and 160/230, so you know how far you still have to go before you start gambling.
  7. Take your time lining up. Aiming is by holding down, so you can adjust the angle before you let go, and there is no rush between shots.

Steady clears beat flashy shots here. Protect the red line first, then build combos once the board has some breathing room.

Best strategy for high scores

The biggest jump in my results came from combos. My early runs ended at 3,250 and 21,500 points. The run that reached 50,150 came from picking shots that cleared a group every time, so the Combo counter kept climbing instead of resetting.

A good habit is to scan the board for the largest cluster that hangs from only a few bubbles. Popping the bubbles that hold it up drops the whole section in one shot, and that is where the big score jumps came from.

What to do when the board gets crowded

Every missed shot adds bubbles and pushes the formation toward the red dashed line, so a crowded board is mostly a result of a few misses in a row. When it starts to get tight, stop going for long combos and clear the lowest bubbles first to buy back space.

Once there is room again, go back to hunting combos. Playing safe for two or three shots is cheaper than losing a run that was going well.
